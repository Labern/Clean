Focus — sign-in and accounts

An email is the one thing Focus asks for. You sign in with a six-digit code sent to it. There are no passwords and no phone number. A name and a photo are optional, added whenever you like. One screen to get in, then out of the way.

Principles

Built on a proven service, not hand-made

Sign-in codes, sessions and token rotation come from a mature, audited authentication service. We write the product around it, never our own cryptography.

The least data possible

Required: email. Optional: first name, surname, photo. Nothing else is asked for, inferred or stored: no phone, no address, no birthday, no tracking.

Fewest clicks

Type your email, press Return, paste the code. Focus fills it in and signs you in on the sixth digit. A link in the email does the same in one click.

Yours to leave

Sign out anywhere. Download your data, or delete your account in one step. Deleting erases it, it doesn't just hide it.

How it would be built

Identity and data: Supabase (London region)

Supabase Auth sends the six-digit email codes and manages sessions: single-use codes with short expiry and rate limits, and refresh tokens that rotate, with reuse detection. Postgres row-level security means each person can only ever read their own row. It's open source, so Focus is never locked in, and hosting in London keeps data in the UK.

Payments: Stripe (already planned)

Stripe holds every card and billing detail. We never see them. A signed Stripe webhook, verified on our server, records the plan against the account. Checkout opens with the email filled in.

Email: Postmark, from paradoxica.uk

Codes go through a dedicated email service with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up for paradoxica.uk, so they arrive in the inbox, not spam, within seconds. The emails are plain and branded, with no tracking pixels.

The Mac app: native, no web views

A small Swift client talks to Supabase over HTTPS. Its tokens are kept in the macOS Keychain, on this device only, never in plain settings. Pro is unlocked by a signed licence that the app checks with a public key built into it, so it can't be faked by editing a settings file. It keeps working offline for 30 days.

Macs per person

Each Mac gets a random ID when it signs in. Nothing about the hardware is collected. Pro covers your Macs up to the limit you choose (2–3). Signing in on one more lets you sign an old one out in the same screen, so nobody is ever locked out of what they paid for.

Who builds what

Focus Site: the Supabase project, the email templates, the Stripe webhook, signed licences, the site's Download and Account pages. Focus App: the sign-in screens, Keychain storage, checking the licence, the Account page, and the Mac limit. One shared spec in the repo, so the two halves agree.

Why not the alternatives: Clerk and Auth0 are excellent but hosted outside the UK and keep more profile data by default. WorkOS is the one to add later for Enterprise single sign-on (Google, Okta), and it sits alongside this rather than replacing it.

Security, written down before code

  • Codes: 6 digits, single use, expire in 10 minutes, at most 5 tries, then a fresh code. Rate-limited per email and per network. Stored hashed, never in plain text.
  • Sessions: short-lived access tokens (1 hour) and rotating refresh tokens. If an old refresh token is reused, every session for that account is signed out.
  • On the Mac: tokens live in the Keychain, on this device only. Nothing sensitive goes in preferences or logs.
  • Licences: signed on the server with a key that never leaves it. The app holds only the public half, so a copied or edited licence fails.
  • Server: every table locked by row-level security. The service key exists only on the server. Webhooks are verified by their signature, and repeated deliveries are handled safely.
  • Before launch: a written threat model, automated tests for every sign-in path (including expired codes, reused tokens and a wrong code six times), and an independent review of the server rules.
