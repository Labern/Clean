From Manager
to Director.

Ten steps, two to five years, written for the UK firm. The jump is not a harder version of your current job — it is a change of category. Managers are paid to deliver; Directors are paid to originate and to own a number. Everything below is built around that discontinuity and the UK calendar that gates it.

1

DeclareSay it out loud, in writing, this cycle

Ambition kept private does not exist. Put Director in your LEAD goals and say it to your counsellor before 1 July — then ask the question most people avoid: what exactly is missing? A pipeline you are not named in never considers you.

Ask for the gap list. Get it in writing.
2

NarrowOwn one intersection, not five

Pick a sector × capability tight enough that you are the first call — not "data", but "AI assurance for insurers". Directors are bought, not applied for: the market has to ask for you by name. Breadth cannot be sold; depth can.

Target: top-3 recognised name in one niche
3

SponsorRecruit the person who spends capital on you

A mentor advises you; a sponsor argues for you in rooms you will never enter — roughly 23% better odds. You sit in the UK and global head office, so the deciders are in your building, alongside 8,000 others. Proximity pays only if they can name what you did.

1 sponsor + 3–5 partners who can cite your numbers
4

MixMove from 80/20 delivery to 60/40 by year two

Being superb at delivery is the most common way to get trapped in it. The firm has every incentive to keep you billing, and nobody will hand you origination time. Block it like a client meeting, and defend it like one.

~2 days a week on origination by end of Y2
5

RevenueGet a number with your name on it

Directors are judged on owned revenue and pipeline, not effort. Move from "I delivered on that account" to "that account is mine", and track it monthly. Director bands sit near ~$1.75m managed, with a matching cross-sell target.

Own the P&L of one account or capability
6

Cross-sellBring the whole firm — unless you are in Audit

Outside Assurance, pulling Tax, SaT and Consulting into your accounts is the Director test. Inside it, UK operational separation inverts this: the ring-fenced audit practice takes no fee for introductions, and pay turns above all on audit quality. Know which currency you are paid in.

Advisory: 3+ service lines  ·  Audit: quality + tenders
7

BenchBuild the person who replaces you

You cannot be promoted out of a seat nobody else can fill. Grow two Managers who can run delivery without you, and let partners watch them do it. Successor risk is a quiet reason strong cases get deferred a year.

2 named successors, proven on live engagements
8

CaseWrite the business case three years early

Submission windows are often a single week — the case is retrieved then, not written. Keep a live one-pager led by attribution: "Led the pricing review that recovered £4.2m", never "was involved in". Committees credit you less than you assume.

Refresh quarterly, before every counsellor conversation
9

HeadroomGo where the UK firm is actually growing

The firm must believe the seat pays for itself. The chart above is that argument: Strategy & Transactions ran +10% last year while Consulting fell 6% and shed ~150 senior roles. Seats are funded out of growth, not need.

Aim at a line that grew, not one that cut
10

DeadlineSet a decision date and honour it

Give it two full cycles once you are formally in the pipeline. If you are not nominated, not sponsored, and the answer never becomes specific, that is the answer. A lateral at the next grade resets both cases at once.

Hard review point: two cycles, then decide
Gate 1 — Personal case
Can you do the job? Delivery excellence, ratings, leadership, a visible niche. Necessary, and on its own never sufficient.
Gate 2 — Business case
Does the seat pay for itself? Owned revenue, pipeline, cross-sell, a market with funded headroom.
Gate 3 — Political case
Who argues for you in the room? One sponsor with capital, several partners who can quote your numbers.

Scale of the prize. £3.78bn of UK fee income in FY25, 59 new equity partners admitted, an average £787k distributed per partner. The clock: two to four years a grade — compressing it means over-delivering on gates 2 and 3 before anyone asks. The failure mode is never effort: it is a brilliant deliverer with no book, no sponsor and no niche, waiting to be noticed by a process that does not work that way.
