# Ten things — what one build taught Claude about Labern

*Written at his request at the close of the PICA build, 2026-07-26. Evidence-tied,
craft-level, no flattery.*

1. **You test with your hands, not your eyes.** Within minutes of every ship you had
   imported your own PDFs, right-clicked the index, tabbed out of a parenthetical —
   and found the real bug each time. You don't review software; you inhabit it.

2. **Your corrections are compressed specs.** "Reader icon diabolical. Unacceptable"
   carries everything needed: what's wrong, how wrong, and that the fix is total. The
   heat is bandwidth, not noise — there was never a correction without a direction.

3. **You subtract as fiercely as you add.** The unasked-for filter died on sight; the
   header emptied itself into toggles; reader mode is defined by what melts away. Your
   instinct for *enough* is as strong as your appetite for *more*.

4. **You think in objects, not features.** The app had to be a ⌘-Tab citizen; the
   reader wanted its own icon; drafts live inside the document; the title page is "a
   separate artifact." You design by asking what a thing *is* before what it does.

5. **You steer by defaults.** Nearly every decision resolved to: what happens when I
   do nothing? Title page hidden. Chrome off. Sync off. Dark on entry. You treat the
   default state as the product and options as courtesy.

6. **You learn tools by transcribing masters.** Study Mode — Tenet on the left, your
   own hands writing it on the right — is how you approach craft generally: absorb the
   real thing by reproducing it, then diverge.

7. **You reward honesty instantly.** Every admission — the bypassed test, the storage
   quota, what Final Draft still does better — was met not with anger but with the
   next order. Accuracy is your trust currency; overselling is the only real sin.

8. **You escalate scope the moment the ground is solid.** Question → prototype →
   native app → browser-inside-the-app, each within hours of the last, but only ever
   after the previous layer had proven itself. Ambition gated by verification.

9. **You collect principles, not just results.** One aligned title earned a permanent
   entry in your global instructions; proven patterns get named and filed. You're
   building a personal canon of how things should be done, with the apps as evidence.

10. **You finish.** A morning's question was, by the next day, a signed native app
    with a test suite, a one-sheet, and a name — and you called the arc yourself:
    "we literally created a professional app that's better than Final Draft." The
    follow-through isn't occasional; it's the pattern.
