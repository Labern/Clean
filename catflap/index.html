Flap Guard

An add-on for the cat flap you already have, about £22 in parts. Any chipped cat can go out except the two kittens. Coming in is never blocked.

Mock-up: the flap seen from inside the house. Everything is on the inside face, and nothing goes outside where the weather is.

Do we need the big cats' chip numbers?

No. The system only needs to know the two kittens. The rule is:

The flap is locked for going out by default. Any chip it reads unlocks it, unless that chip is Misato's or Pen Pen's.

So the big cats never get enrolled. Their chips work as keys just because they aren't kittens. A neighbour's cat that wandered in can also get back out. The kittens are learned the way SureFlap does it: press the button, hold the kitten at the tunnel, and the reader stores the ID. The number on the vet card is the same number the reader outputs, so you can also type it in.

Cat microchipping has been compulsory in England since June 2024, so the big cats are very likely chipped. A vet scans them for free if you want to be sure. An unchipped cat is the one case this design locks in.

This works because of the point you made: big cats go out head-first. The head enters the tunnel, the shoulders follow, and the chip between the shoulder blades passes through the coil. The flap only needs to unlock when the cat pushes, and by then the chip has been read. SureFlap uses the same principle.

If a read is missed, the flap stays locked. The worst outcome is a big cat waiting a second and pushing again. A kitten can never get out because a read failed.

What's in it, and what each part does

£0already in the cats

The microchip

A grain-of-rice glass capsule under the skin between the shoulder blades. It has no battery. When a 134.2 kHz magnetic field passes over it, the field powers it for a moment and it sends back its 15-digit ID (standard: ISO FDX-B).

incl.the antenna

The coil

A loop of copper wire. The reader drives a current through it, and that makes the magnetic field that wakes the chip. Range is short, about 3–8 cm with the cheap module, so the coil sits at the tunnel where every cat has to pass within reach.

£8WL-134 module

The chip reader

A small board that drives the coil, listens for the chip's reply and decodes it. It sends the ID out over two wires as plain text (serial). It doesn't decide anything. It just reports which chip it saw.

£4ESP32-C3 SuperMini

The brain

A thumbnail-sized computer with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. It reads the IDs, applies the rule, tells the servo where to go, and remembers the kittens' IDs when power is lost. Over Wi-Fi it can send your phone a ping and later talk to Ring or Home Assistant.

£3MG90S

The servo

A tiny motor with a gearbox, a position sensor and its own control chip, all in one block. It takes a pulse every 20 ms. The length of the pulse (1–2 ms) sets the angle (0–180°). It turns to that angle and holds it there. Ours only uses two angles: bolt in and bolt out.

£5USB plug + 470 µF cap

Power

5 V from any phone charger runs everything. The capacitor is a small reservoir. The servo pulls a spike of current when it moves, and without the capacitor that spike can reset the ESP32.

Plus wires, a small project box and hot glue (≈£2). Total ≈ £22.

Using your four clips

Your flap has two clips that block in and two that block out. The design keeps that mechanism, because it's already shaped to stop the flap:

ClipsTodayWith Flap Guard
"Block in" pairManualLeft open for good. Coming in is never restricted.
"Block out" pairManualMoved by the servo. A stiff wire pushrod from the servo arm to one clip slides it shut or open. If that's awkward, the servo carries its own bolt into the same spot.

One engineering rule: the servo moves the bolt, and the frame takes the push. The clip or bolt has to sit in a slot, so a cat leaning on the flap pushes against plastic, not against the servo's gears. The clips already work this way, which is another reason to reuse them.

Build order

  1. Bench test the reader. Wire the WL-134 to the ESP32 and print what it reads. Hold each cat near the coil and see the 15-digit IDs come out. This also tells you the real range with your cats' chips. Kittens often have mini chips, which read a little shorter.
  2. Bench test the servo. Two angles, bolt in and bolt out. Find them by hand on the clip.
  3. Fit the coil. For the first version, tape the stock coil to the roof of the tunnel on the inside face, since heads and shoulders pass right under it. Upgrade: wind a loop around the whole tunnel, as in the mock-up.
  4. Fit the servo under the tunnel on the inside, with the pushrod to the "block out" clip.
  5. Enrol the kittens. Hold the button, present Misato, then repeat for Pen Pen. The LED flashes to confirm each one.
  6. Watch a day of traffic. Every read is logged and every kitten attempt pings your phone through ntfy (the same app the kittens tracker already uses).

Later

A magnet and reed switch on the flap (£1) tells it when a cat actually went through. That gives you "big cat out 14:02 / in 16:40" logs and lets it relock the instant the flap closes instead of after 4 s. From there, Ring and Home Assistant are just more messages from the ESP32.
