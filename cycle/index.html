🌈THE BREAK
day 1 — bleeding starts, the storm lifts

In the last stretch of the luteal phase, progesterone and oestrogen fall off a cliff together — that withdrawal is what makes moods most volatile roughly five days before menstruation, and keeps them stormy right up to bleeding. The moment it begins, hormones bottom out and stabilise, and the mood breaks like weather: within a day or two of day 1, calm returns. 🌈

📖 what each phase is doing

Menstrual 🌈

days 1–5
Biology
The uterine lining sheds. Oestrogen and progesterone sit at their lowest — and that hormonal floor is exactly why the storm breaks.
Mood
The break. The luteal storm lifts within a day or two of bleeding starting — irritability drains away, calm and clarity return. Tired, but fine.
Body
Cramps, heavier fatigue on days 1–2, headaches, lower-back ache.

Follicular ☀️

days 6–12
Biology
FSH recruits a new egg follicle and oestrogen climbs steadily, rebuilding the uterine lining. (Strictly this phase begins on day 1 and overlaps the period — shown here from the period’s end.)
Mood
The best stretch: energy, optimism, focus and sociability all rise with oestrogen.
Body
Energy returns, skin clears, sleep steadies, workouts feel easier.

Ovulation 🌟

days 13–15
Biology
A surge of LH releases the egg — reliably about 14 days before the next period, whatever the cycle length. Oestrogen peaks, with a little testosterone bump.
Mood
Radiant peak: confidence, charm, verbal fluency and libido at their highest.
Body
Possible one-sided twinge (mittelschmerz), clear stretchy discharge, slight temperature rise afterwards.

Luteal ⛈ — the one to watch

days 16–28
Biology
The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum and pumps out progesterone. With no pregnancy, progesterone and oestrogen collapse over the final ~5 days — the crash behind PMS.
Mood
A gradually shortening fuse: irritability, anxiety, tearfulness, low patience. ⚠ The last five days are the danger zone — worst at their start, five days before bleeding — then it breaks on day 1. 🌈
Body
Bloating, breast tenderness, cravings, spots, poorer sleep, lower energy.
🖤 when it’s more than PMS — PMDD

PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder) is the severe form of the luteal mood dip — it affects roughly 3–8% of people who menstruate. The hormones themselves behave normally; the brain is unusually sensitive to the progesterone-and-oestrogen crash of the final luteal days, which disturbs serotonin.

The pattern is the same shape as this app’s danger zone, but far deeper: not a short fuse so much as despair, rage, severe anxiety, hopelessness, feeling out of control — strong enough to disrupt work and relationships — arriving in the last luteal week and switching off within a day or two of bleeding, exactly like the break, followed by a fully symptom-free stretch after the period.

What helps: it is diagnosable — symptoms tracked daily across two or more cycles, which is precisely what this calendar’s notes and mood tags record — and very treatable: SSRIs (often taken during the luteal phase only), hormonal options, and cycle-aware planning. If the danger zone here reads as despair rather than irritability, take that pattern to a GP — it has a name, and it responds to treatment.
