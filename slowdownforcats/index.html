Slow down
for cats.

Around 630 cats a day are hit on UK roads. Most of it happens after dark, on the street they live on.

The numbers

Somebody's cat, every two and a half minutes.

Nobody officially counts them, which is part of the problem. These are the best figures there are.

If you drive

Four habits. No extra time.

Cats don't read traffic. They freeze in headlights, then bolt. Speed decides whether you can react.

  • Ease off on residential streets

    That's where cats live and cross. Going 20 instead of 30 roughly halves the distance you need to stop.

  • Dusk to dawn is peak time

    Cats hunt and roam most in low light, which is exactly when drivers see least.

  • Watch for eyeshine

    Two small green-gold dots at kerb height are a cat looking at you. Expect it to move.

  • Brake, don't swerve

    Slow down in a straight line. A swerve into oncoming traffic or a pavement helps nobody.

If it happens

Stop. It matters to someone.

  1. Pull over where it's safe and put your hazards on. Don't put yourself in the road.
  2. Approach slowly. An injured cat may lash out, so wrap it in a coat or towel before you lift it.
  3. Get it to the nearest vet, even if it isn't yours. They'll stabilise it and scan for a microchip, and most won't charge you.
  4. If it hasn't survived, move it off the road if it's safe to, check for a collar tag, and report it to the local council so it can be scanned and the owner told.
  5. Knock on a few doors. Nearly every cat on a street belongs to someone on that street.

The Highway Code (Rule 286) asks drivers to report any collision with an animal and to try to find the owner of a pet.4 It isn't the law for cats yet. It's still the right thing to do.

If you have a cat

Give them the odds.

  • In at night

    Feed in the evening and keep them in until morning. It cuts out the most dangerous hours.

  • Chip, then check the details

    A chip only helps if the database has your current phone number.

  • Reflective, quick-release collar

    Headlights pick it up, and it snaps open if it ever catches on something.

  • Neuter

    Neutered cats roam far less, so they cross far fewer roads.
